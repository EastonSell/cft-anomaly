\title{Finite thermodynamic reconstruction of an integer-spectrum CFT}
\begin{abstract}
For a compact unitary two-dimensional CFT with $c_L=c_R=12k$, integer
total dimensions and spins, and unmultiplied modular invariance, finitely
many exact angular moments at finitely many temperatures determine the
torus partition function. We prove explicit sampling bounds using
separate $\Gamma(2)$ invariance. The angular order is optimal: for every
$k$ we construct two genuine Narain CFTs with identical lower moments
at every temperature but different moments at the stated order.
The result determines the torus spectrum, not operator products; it
assumes exact data and does not establish numerical stability.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

\noindent\textbf{Thermal data.}
The torus trace encodes energy and angular momentum, but its zero-angle
restriction sums over spin. Modular spectral reconstruction and
discreteness have substantial literature \cite{ashrafi,kaidi,benjamin}.
Integer-energy partition functions and their thermal chiralization were
studied by Ashrafi and Loran \cite{ashrafi}. Dymarsky and Shapere
exhibited Narain CFTs with identical thermal traces but distinct angular
traces \cite{codes}; Kaidi and Perlmutter discussed earlier CFT pairs
with the same light spectrum \cite{kaidi}. Benjamin \emph{et al.}
studied reconstruction from light, scalar and spin-one spectra
\cite{benjamin}, assuming a simple cuspidal eigenspectrum. We give a finite-sampling
consequence of integer dimensions and full modular invariance.

Fix the spatial circle to circumference $2\pi$ and write
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 Z(z+it,-z+it)&=\Tr e^{-2\pi t(\Delta-k)+2\pi izJ},\\
 M_r(t)&=\Tr[J^r e^{-2\pi t(\Delta-k)}].
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
Here $t=\beta/(2\pi)>0$ is the rescaled inverse temperature and the
moments include descendants. Assume a
compact unitary bosonic CFT with discrete finite-multiplicity spectrum,
a unique vacuum, integer $\Delta=h+\bar h$ and $J=h-\bar h$,
$c_L=c_R=12k$ for $k\in\Z_{>0}$, and simultaneous unmultiplied
$S,T$ invariance. Neither parity invariance nor rationality is assumed.

\noindent\textbf{Theorem.}
Two theories satisfying these hypotheses for the same $k$ have identical
torus partition functions if their $M_r$ agree for every $0\le r\le R_k$
at either $k$ distinct inverse temperatures all in $(0,1]$, or all in
$[1,\infty)$, or $2k-1$ arbitrary distinct positive inverse temperatures, where
\begin{equation}
 R_k=\begin{cases}3k,&k\text{ even},\\3k-1,&k\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
These data are exact; equality determines spin-resolved state
multiplicities, not OPE coefficients.
Alternatively choose $k$ distinct $t>1$, use all for $r=0$, and use
any $k-\lceil r/3\rceil+1$ of them at each $r\ge1$.

\noindent\textbf{Proof.}
Continue the trace holomorphically to independent $\tau,\sigma$ in the
upper half-plane, with physical slice $\sigma=-\bar\tau$. Normal
convergence and holomorphic uniqueness on this maximally totally real
slice continue simultaneous modular invariance. Since $2h,2\bar h\in\Z$, shifting either variable by $2$
has no phase: $e^{4\pi i(h-k/2)}=1$. Conjugating independent $T^2$
by simultaneous $S$ gives separate $\Gamma(2)$ invariance.

For fixed $\sigma$, the left infinity cusp has pole order at most $k$
in the width-two coordinate $u=e^{\pi i\tau}$. At the other cusps,
simultaneous modular invariance moves that cusp to infinity and
sends $\sigma$ to another fixed point of the upper half-plane; the
same absolute trace bound applies. The right-variable statement follows
identically. Since $X(2)$ is the $\lambda$-sphere with cusps
$0,1,\infty$, each trace has
\begin{equation}
 Z(\tau,\sigma)=\frac{P(x,y)}{[x(1-x)y(1-y)]^k},
 \quad x=\lambda(\tau),\ y=\lambda(\sigma),
 \label{eq:form}
\end{equation}
with $\deg_xP,\deg_yP\le3k$. This genus-zero statement does not assume
rationality; see also the level-two discussion in \cite{bae}. To obtain
the two-variable form, evaluate at $3k+1$ independent first-variable
points: the resulting coefficient functions inherit the right cusp
bounds as finite linear combinations of traces.

Let $P$ be the difference of the two numerators and put $n=3k$.
The map $t\mapsto\lambda(it)$ is strictly monotone from $1$ to $0$, and
\begin{equation}
 \lambda'(\tau)=\pi i\lambda(\tau)(1-\lambda(\tau))
 \vartheta_3(\tau)^4\ne0\quad(\tau=it).
 \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
Suppose inductively $P=(x-y)^rQ_r$. The $r$th angular derivative at
$z=0$ is a nonzero factor times $q_r(x)=Q_r(x,x)$, with
$\deg q_r\le2(n-r)$. Thus vanishing moments give zeros of $q_r$.
Simultaneous modular transformations act on $x,y$ by the same $S_3$
M\"obius transformation. The denominator in \eqref{eq:form} and
$(x-y)^r$ acquire nonzero section factors, so a zero propagates through
its orbit. A generic orbit has six finite points, exactly two in
$(0,1)$, namely $x,1-x$; the exceptional orbit is $\{1/2,-1,2\}$.
The samples meet at least $k$ orbits and give at least $6k-3$ distinct
zeros, exceeding $2(n-r)$ for $r\ge2$.

For $r=0,1$ use the common vacuum. Near the diagonal infinity cusp,
the vacuum cancels and the remaining terms have total degree at least
two in $(u,v)$, by integer $\Delta$ and unitarity. Thus $P$ has joint
order at least two at every diagonal cusp, the other two following
from modular covariance. As a section, $q_0$ has two additional zeros
at each cusp and $q_1$ has one at each after division by $x-y$; a zero
at infinity is a deficit from the maximum polynomial degree. Together
with the orbit zeros, this forces $q_r=0$ for $r=0,1$ too. Thus
$x-y$ divides $Q_r$ and the induction continues.

For even $k$, data through $r=3k$ force $(x-y)^{3k+1}\mid P$,
impossible by bidegree unless $P=0$. For odd $k$, data through
$r=3k-1$ leave only $P=C(x-y)^{3k}$; simultaneous $S$ sends $x,y$ to
$1-x,1-y$, fixing the denominator and reversing this odd polynomial,
so $C=0$. For reduced sampling every strictly one-sided sample has a
distinct six-point orbit; the cusp zeros suffice at $r=0$, and for
$r\ge1$ the stated count $N_r$ obeys $6N_r>6k-2r$.

\noindent\textbf{Low-energy corollary.}
The complete spin-resolved state spectrum through
$N=\lfloor3k/2\rfloor$ determines $Z$. Indeed, for a difference with
this jet zero, the cleared numerator has order at least $2(N+1)$ at
each diagonal cusp. After $r$ divisions by $x-y$, its diagonal
restriction has degree $6k-2r$ and at least
$3[2(N+1)-r]$ cusp zeros. Their number exceeds the degree for every
$r\le3k$, forcing the difference to vanish. The physical pairs below
first differ at $\Delta=N$, proving this cutoff sharp.

\noindent\textbf{Physical seeds.}
Let $U,V$ be the $D_{4,1}$ vacuum and vector characters; the spinor
characters also equal $V$ at zero chemical potentials. Define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A&=U(\tau)U(\sigma)+3V(\tau)V(\sigma),\\
 W&=U(\tau)V(\sigma)-V(\tau)U(\sigma).
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:AW}
\end{equation}
The $E_{8,1}$ character is $U^2+3V^2$. Hence the genuine
$E_{8,1}\times D_{4,1}$ and $D_{4,1}^3$ theories have difference
$3W^2A$. Their thermal equality follows from the $D_4^2$ versus $E_8$
example in \cite{ashrafi}, after tensoring with $D_4$. They have equal
$M_0,M_1$ but unequal $M_2$ at every
finite $t$.

For later composition use a neighboring pair instead: diagonal
$D_{8,1}\times D_{4,1}$ and $D_{4,1}^3$. The $D_8$ diagonal
characters give
\begin{equation}
 Z_{D_8\times D_4}-Z_{D_4^3}=W^2A.
 \label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
The $D_8$ root lattice is an index-two extension of $D_4^2$ by the
vector--vector class. The corresponding maximal isotropic gluing codes
therefore meet in codimension one. A concrete independent certificate
is supplied with this source.

For a $k=2$ neighboring pair, take the $D_4^6\oplus D_4(-1)^6$
discriminant group $(\F_2^2)^{12}$. Consecutive bit pairs encode its
twelve classes, the first six left-moving. Let $v_i$ be
\begin{align*}
 &(4280689,4003343,13490061,15943387,\\
 &4783610,4517512,8704049,3143493,\\
 &9304945,11135812,2974764,11955798),
\end{align*}
indexed from $i=0$. Set $a=v_1$, $a'=5138$, $f=30$, and let $S$ be
spanned by $v_i+\operatorname{bit}_i(f)v_1$ for $i\ne1$. Then
$S+\langle a\rangle$ and $S+\langle a'\rangle$ are neighboring
maximal isotropic codes. The supplied standard-library certificate
checks their $2^{11}$-element intersection, both $2^{12}$-element
spans, the quadratic and bilinear forms, and the grouped weight
enumerators. The latter give exactly
\begin{equation}
 Z_{S+\langle a\rangle}-Z_{S+\langle a'\rangle}=W^6.
 \label{eq:k2}
\end{equation}
The initial lattice has determinant $4^{12}=2^{24}$; adjoining
either code gives index $2^{12}$ and an even unimodular lattice of
signature $(24,24)$. These define genuine compact unitary Narain
CFTs \cite{narain,codes}. The vacuum is unique; isotropy gives
integer spin and, since all three nonzero $D_4$ classes have norm
$1\bmod2$, integer total dimension. At the first changed level
$\Delta=3$, the sixth state-spin moments differ by
$8^6 6!=188{,}743{,}680$.

\noindent\textbf{Sharpness for every $k$.}
We use the following elementary composition of neighboring isotropic
codes. Write two pairs as $C=S+\langle a\rangle$,
$C'=S+\langle a'\rangle$ and $D=T+\langle b\rangle$,
$D'=T+\langle b'\rangle$, where the lifts are isotropic and the
polarized pairings satisfy $(a,a')=(b,b')=1$ in $\F_2$.
In the orthogonal direct sum define
\begin{align}
 E_+&=S\oplus T+\langle(a,b),(a',b')\rangle,\nonumber\\
 E_-&=S\oplus T+\langle(a,b'),(a',b)\rangle .
 \label{eq:bell}
\end{align}
The two added vectors are isotropic and mutually orthogonal because
$1+1=0$. Both codes are maximal isotropic and they are neighbors:
their common hyperplane is generated by $S\oplus T$ and
$(a+a',b+b')$. Comparing their four cosets gives
\begin{equation}
 Z_{E_+}-Z_{E_-}=(Z_C-Z_{C'})(Z_D-Z_{D'}).
 \label{eq:product}
\end{equation}
Iterating \eqref{eq:bell} with the two physical seeds produces even
unimodular Narain pairs whose partition-function differences are
$W^{3k}$ for even $k$ and $W^{3k-1}A$ for odd $k$. They have equal
moments through $R_k-1$ for all $t$, while the $R_k$th differs. Indeed,
the character identity $\lambda=2V/(U+V)$ and \eqref{eq:lambda} imply
that $W$ has a simple angular zero: $U(it)>0$ and $\lambda'(it)\ne0$.
Also $A(it,it)>0$.
Thus \eqref{eq:R} is physically sharp for every $k$. These pairs first
differ at $\Delta=\lfloor3k/2\rfloor$, so the low-energy corollary is
sharp as well. The certificate also explicitly checks the first composed
$k=3$ pair.

\noindent\textbf{Scope.}
The theorem concerns exact torus data and makes no numerical stability
claim. Positive integral modular character data alone do not supply
sewing or crossing; examples are known \cite{fakez}. Our sharpness
pairs are actual lattice CFTs, not merely formal spectra. The $k=2$
seed was found by a finite search and exactly certified. A complete
literature and priority assessment remains open.
